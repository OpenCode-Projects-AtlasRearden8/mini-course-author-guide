# Drip Announcements with a Plain Newsletter

You do not need a marketing machine. You need three small emails, sent to
a plain newsletter list, on a schedule you can keep. This chapter is about
the announcement drip, the buyer welcome drip, and why a simple tool
beats a big one.

## Plain beats powerful

A "plain newsletter" means: an email service that sends text and simple
links, with an archive and an unsubscribe link. Not a funnel builder, not
an automation suite with 40 settings. Reasons, in order of importance:

- **You will actually use it.** Tools with forty features get abandoned
  by authors; tools that feel like email get used weekly.
- **Plain email survives spam filters.** Heavy-designed HTML campaigns
  trip filters; a warm plain-text note from a real name does not.
- **Nothing to learn before shipping.** The launch waits on the writing,
  not on a learning curve.

Any of the small, boring providers will do for a v1 list (a simple
provider with mailing-list features at a few dollars a month, up to
about 1,000 subscribers). Choose one, set it up in under an hour, move
on. You can migrate later; lists export.

## The one rule of your list

**Nobody lands on your list without a yes.** A visible signup that says
what they get and how often. Then:

- The signup form says: "Emails about once or twice a week — course
  notes, honest launches, occasionally nothing useful. Unsubscribe any
  time."
- Email frequency 1× or 2× per week, never 0 and never 5. Keepable beats
  impressive; a missed month costs trust.

## The launch drip: three emails to your readers

Write these before launch day. Save them as drafts. The "When" days are
suggestions, not law:

| # | When | Purpose | Sketch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Launch day | Announce, promise, link | "I built a small lesson that gets you ______. It's $49, it's plain, and it works. Here's the page." |
| 2 | Day 3 | Prove, not pressure | Answer the one real question; show the printable or a real result; repeat term of refund; one link, once. |
| 3 | Day 7 | Last plain note | "Doors are nothing special, so this is the last time I mention it. Here's the link if it's useful. Back to normal letters." |

Sketch rule: **one link per email.** Multiple links dilute the sole
action; one link respects the reader's deciding brainpower.

### Email 1 draft (adapt, do not worship)

> Subject: A small lesson: ______
>
> Hi —
>
> Quick one, and I'll keep it short. I turned the ______ part of my
> manual into a small lesson you can finish in a week of short sittings:
> you'll be able to ______ on your own in about ______ minutes.
>
> It's $49, it comes with a ______ checklist you can print, and if it
> isn't for you there's a no-questions 14-day refund.
>
> Here's the page: <link>
>
> Either way, thanks for reading —
> Dylan

## The buyer welcome drip: three emails to buyers

Buyers get automatic-ish emails, one at purchase and a couple after, all
plain and short. Their job is *completion of the lesson*, not upselling
you:

| # | When | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | At purchase | Access details, the start-here link, the printable PDF, the promise sentence restated, the 14-day policy reminder. |
| B | ~Day 3 | "How's it going?" — restart command for sitting one, link to the checkpoint they may have missed. |
| C | ~Day 7 | The finish line check: "You should now be able to ______." Ask for a reply with their result — replies tell you the truth and warm the relationship. |

## Deliverability, the friendly minimum

- **Send a welcome email immediately** on signup (any provider does
  this). It confirms you are a real sender and the signup worked.
- **One CTA per email.** One link, one idea, one ask.
- **Plain text or very simple formatting.** If a template has more
  blocks than an author writes, skip the template.
- **Your name in the from line.** "Dylan" beats "ASDALE DYNAMICS."

## What a giant tool would have given you that week (nothing)

Multi-step automations, tag trees, scoring, A/B testing at 8 subscribers
— none of it moves a mini-course launch. The three-email drip moves it.
Start plain. Complexity is a legitimate buy at a list of thousands, not
at a list of two hundred honest readers.

## Exercise (15 minutes)

1. Choose your provider and create the list with the plain signup.
2. Write Email 1 (announce) using the draft above and your blanks.
3. Write Emails B and C of the buyer drip — the two emails most authors
   skip and readers most appreciate.
4. Schedule Email 1 for launch day; park 2 and 3 as drafts with dates.
5. Put "write next week's plain email" on your calendar weekly, 30
   minutes. It is the only marketing meeting you will ever hold.
