# Lesson Pages and Printables

Your lesson page is the course. Videos support it; the page *is* it. This
chapter gives you the page structure that hands the reader value before
they pay, and the printable that makes the promise work on paper.

## The page before the paywall

You need two pages with the same skeleton: a **free lesson page** (the
storefront proof) and the **lesson inside the course**. The free one
delivers real value — not a trailer — so the buyer trusts the paid one.

The skeleton, in order:

1. **Promise line.** The sentence from chapter one, edited for the person
   who just arrived from a search or a link.
2. **Finish line picture.** "Here is what you will have when this is
   done" — a screenshot of the end state, or the filled-in printable.
3. **Who this is for / not for.** Two short lists. Honesty here reduces
   refunds later; see [the refunds chapter](06-refunds-with-grace.html).
4. **The lesson.** Outline steps as headings, each with its video, its
   one-sentence summary, and the "you should now see" checkpoint.
5. **The printable** (free one here), attached near the step it serves.
6. **What is not included.** One paragraph. Set the border of the promise.
7. **Next step.** For the free page: the buy link. For the paid page: how
   the reader keeps momentum.

A runnable example of this exact skeleton, with the fill-in blanks, is the
[lesson template](lesson-template.html). Copy it, fill it, ship it.

## Checkpoints beat comprehension quizzes

After each video, one line, worded exactly like the template's checkpoint:
"You should now see [the exact expected screen]. If not, check [the likely
fix]." This is the highest-value sentence in course design for beginners.
It converts passive watching into self-correction, and it prevents the
3 a.m. support email of a viewer stuck on step two of nine.

## The printable: one sheet, one job

A printable is a page the reader can print, fill by hand, and pin up. One
sheet per lesson. Its job is to carry the promise off the screen. Good
shapes:

- **Checklist** for a procedure with real steps (setup, launch, weekly
  tasks).
- **Worksheet** with slight blanks to fill (the promise sentence, the pace
  table).
- **Cheat sheet** when the lesson teaches commands or settings.

Design rules, learned the hard way:

- Print it yourself on a real printer before shipping. Margins eat design;
  grayscale eats color meaning. Design that survives grayscale wins.
- Use the whole width, large text, and check boxes big enough for a shaky
  hand. Tired readers print at 7 p.m.
- Put the printable in **PDF** only. PDFs print the same everywhere, which
  is the entire point. Name it like the promise:
  `local-ai-first-run-checklist.pdf`, not `final-v3-real.pdf`.
- Put the printable near the step it serves, not at the bottom where
  people forget it.

## Hand-delivering value (the difference between page and pitch)

"Hand-deliver" means the free page alone could solve a small version of
the reader's problem. If they never buy, they should still be able to do
something real on your free page. This is the difference between a
storefront and a brochure:

- A brochure says: "Inside: the secrets." → No.
- A storefront-proofs-value page says: "Here is the finish line photo, the
  first video in full, and the checklist. The paid lesson gets you from
  first run to daily use." → Yes.

Guard against the trailer temptation: if your free page deleted its only
complete step, it is a trailer. Restore whole steps until it teaches.

## Where the media lives (self-hosted, plainly)

- **Video files** sit in your own hosting — any static host or object
  storage with a simple HTML5 `<video>` tag is fine at v1. No streaming
  service needed until files are large or viewers are many.
- Keep a private, unguessable folder (a long random path) for paid
  videos. It is not a vault, and that is okay; your promise is honest
  access, not enterprise DRM.
- **Course files** (pages + PDFs) live in one folder you can back up in a
  single command. If you cannot zip the course and email it to yourself,
  it is too scattered.

## Exercise (20 minutes)

1. Copy the lesson template. Fill the promise line and the finish line
   picture (a real screenshot will do).
2. Build the free page with *one complete step* from your outline plus
   its checkpoint sentence.
3. Make the one printable: pick checklist, worksheet, or cheat sheet;
   draft it; print it; fix it; export to PDF.
4. Test everything on your phone. Ninety percent of first views are on
   phones, and a lesson that breaks on phones breaks as a product.
