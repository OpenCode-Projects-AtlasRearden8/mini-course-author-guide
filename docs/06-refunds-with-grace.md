# Refunds Handled Kindly

A refund request is not a betrayal. It is a reader telling you their
reality did not match the promise — and how you answer it is the last
chapter of your customer experience and the first chapter of your
reputation. This chapter gives you the policy, the scripts, and the edge
cases, in plain terms.

## Publish the policy before the first sale

Write it down on the sales page, not buried in a footer. A visible,
generous, plainly-worded refund policy *increases* sales for beginner
products, because it costs you less risk than the fear in the buyer's
head. Yours needs five facts:

1. **The window.** For a small self-hosted course: **14 days**, more
   generous than a weekend and less than the advertised-buyer-forgotten
   month. State the day it starts: "from the day of purchase."
2. **The reason: not required.** "You do not need to explain. A reason
   helps us improve, but refunds are not conditional on reasons."
3. **What you get: everything back**, meaning full refund of the purchase
   price in the original payment method. Never "store credit" on an
   unhappy buyer — a record of "refunded kindly" is worth more than $49.
4. **What we keep: your access stops.** State plainly that the refund
   ends access to the course files. No drama, no lockouts before refund.
5. **How to ask: one email address**, and how fast you respond (a day or
   two; promise what you can keep).

### A policy paragraph you can paste and adapt

> If the lesson is not right for you, email us within 14 days of purchase
> and we will refund the full price to your original payment method — no
> awkward explanations required. Telling us a reason helps us make the
> course better, but you are never obliged to give one. After a refund,
> your course access quietly ends, and you keep the free printable that
> came with the lesson page.

## The reply scripts

**Approving (the common case):**

> Hi <name>, that's an easy yes — I've refunded the full $49; you should
> see it back on the card within 3–5 business days. Your access will end
> quietly on its own. If you ever want to tell me what fell short so the
> lesson gets better, I read every reply. Thanks for giving it a try —
> <Dylan>

**Asking a curiosity question (never pressure):**

> Hi <name>, refunding now — that's the policy and I honor it gladly. If
> anything made it hard to use (too fast, too slow, wrong assumptions
> about your setup), I would genuinely like to know. No reply needed.

**The buyer who wants the content but not the price:** do not refund plus
keep. That is attempted not-allowed failure. Refund, end access, be kind.
Fraud cases are rare at this scale and kindness scales better than
enforcement; the occasional loss is cheaper than a reputation audit.

## Handle two edge cases with grace

- **The near-window ask:** a purchase on day 13 asking on day 15. Honor
  it, and consider phrasing the policy as "14 days — and if you email me
  on day 16, I will probably honor it anyway, because I am a person and
  not a policy engine." Kind rules in public, quiet judgment one-on-one.
- **The serial requester.** Ask your processor or marketplace for a
  refund pattern. If the same name appears repeatedly in refunds, that is
  a fraud pattern for you to notice; handle it case by case, refund
  anyway, and learn as you go.

## Automating the walk-away

If you use a payment processor with an API or a marketplace, refunds can
be one click from the payment dashboard; do it *yourself*, personally. A
refund arriving from an impersonal portal feels different — some buyers
will not even remember the purchase when it arrives. Kind refunds are a
customer service you personally own.

## Why kind refunds pay you

- A refunded buyer can be a future buyer of something more fitting. The
  refund *is* the impression, and it is the strongest possible one.
- They tell a friend the experience was decent. This goes unmeasured but
  it is a real effect.
- Low-friction refunds reduce disputes with card issuers, and disputes
  cost you fees and goodwill both.

1. Draft your five facts (window, reason optional, full refund,
   access-stops, how to ask) using the paste-adapt paragraph.
2. Put the policy near the buy button on the free lesson page, not only
   in the footer of the page.
3. Save the approval reply as a text snippet you can paste, and test
   sending it to yourself to see the tone on the receiving end.
