# Glossary

Small words for a small course. Plain definitions, like a friend would
give them — no marketing-font jargon.

**Buyer welcome drip** — The 2–3 short emails a buyer gets automatically
after purchase (access details; a day-3 "how's it going?"; a day-7
finish-line check). Its job is finishing the lesson, not selling more.

**Checkpoint** — A one-line self-verification after a video: "You should
now see [the exact expected screen]. If not, check [the likely fix]."
Turns passive watching into self-correction and prevents most
stuck-learner emails.

**Completed-lesson trust** — The specific trust a reader grants you after
finishing something small you promised. It is the currency a mini-course
prints; grand unfinished courses earn nothing.

**Deliverability** — Whether your email lands in the inbox instead of the
spam folder. Plain text, real names, one link, and an honest signup help;
image-heavy templates and bought lists hurt.

**Drip** — A short series of emails sent on a schedule rather than all at
once. Two you need: the launch drip (3 emails to readers) and the buyer
welcome drip (3 to buyers). See "newsletter."

**Finish line picture** — A screenshot or photo on the lesson page showing
the end state the promise names, so the reader can see where the lesson
goes before trusting it.

**Finishable pace** — An outline budgeted so a normal reader finishes in
small sittings: 60–90 minutes total, no sitting over ~25 minutes of
watching plus ~10 of doing.

**Hand-delivered value** — Free page content that could solve a small
version of the reader's problem on its own. The test: delete the paywall,
and the free page still teaches something true and complete.

**Launch drip** — The three plain emails that announce a launch: day 0
(announce once, plainly), day 3 (prove, not pressure), day 7 (last
mention). One link each.

**Marketplace (storefront-builder)** — A service that sells your digital
product on your behalf — page, checkout, delivery — for a percentage
around ~10% per sale and no monthly fee (Gumroad-style). You trade margin
for shipping speed. *Rates change; verify current pricing.*

**Mini-course** — One lesson, one promise, hostable as plain files on your
own site. Not "a small version of a big course": a complete thing that
keeps one promise well.

**Newsletter (plain)** — An email list on a simple provider: text, one
link, an archive, an unsubscribe. The anti-giant-tool choice for a v1
launch. Cadence kept is worth more than cadence impressive.

**Payout delay** — The days a venue holds money before paying you.
Marketplaces and platforms often hold longer than direct payment
processors. Usually harmless; check before cash-flow-sensitive launches.

**Platform (course platform)** — A service that hosts course structure,
video, and progress (Teachable/Thinkific-style) for a monthly fee plus a
smaller percentage per sale. Overkill for a mini-course unless you grow
into multi-module teaching. *Rates change; verify current pricing.*

**Printable** — A one-page PDF with one job: carrying the promise onto
paper (checklist, worksheet, or cheat sheet). Print it yourself before
shipping; survive grayscale or lose tired readers.

**Promise-sized lesson** — A lesson that fills the sentence: "After this
lesson, you will be able to ____, on your own, in about ____ minutes."
Both blanks concrete, both checkable.

**Refund window** — The published number of days a no-questions refund is
honored. 14 days is the default for a small self-hosted course — generous
enough to trust, short enough to stop forgotten months.

**Restart command** — The exact sentence at the end of a video naming what
to do next time, with a file, a verb, and a screen to return to. Kills
restart cost, the main killer of completion.

**Self-hosted** — Your static pages plus a payment processor's
percentage-plus-cents fee and a small hosting bill. Highest margin,
needs an afternoon of setup, and you own every file. The default v1.

**Trailer page (anti-pattern)** — A "free lesson" page that contains no
complete step, only previews. Feels like a commercial, converts worse
than a page that teaches, and quietly refunds you trust.
