# Record Without Fancy Gear

You do not need a studio. You need quiet, daylight, and one take with
permission to be plainly human. This chapter is the gear list, the room
check, and the honest tolerances that make recording feel good to watch.

## The gear you already have

- **Camera:** your phone, or your laptop's webcam. Phone beats webcam when
  it is steady and at eye level.
- **Microphone:** your phone or laptop microphone is sufficient *if the room
  is quiet*. Audio quality is driven by the room more than by the mic.
- **Screen capture:** free. On Windows, `Win+G`. On macOS, `Cmd+Shift+5`.
  Both export a screen area with optional mic input.
- **Editing:** trim only. Free players and editors can trim clips; you may
  not need editing at all if you record in short named segments per your
  outline table.

Total new spend for a working setup: **$0**, or a small amount for aused
tripod or laptop stand if your phone keeps wobbling. A phone leaning against
a stack of books works. That is not a joke; I have shipped lessons that way.

## The room check (2 minutes, worth more than any purchase)

1. **Noise:** turn off fans, AC, notifications. Close the door and the
   window over the street. Fridge hum is a killer; shift rooms.
2. **Echo:** clap once. If it rings, you are recording in a nice bathroom.
   Sit where there are soft things — curtains, bedding, a rug, laundry
   baskets through the wall all count — within a few feet.
3. **Light:** face a window. Daylight in front of you beats any lamp behind
   you. Do not sit with a window behind you unless you want the silhouette
   look, which you do not.
4. **Phone at eye level**, arm's length or slightly closer, horizontal for
   screen-side framing.

## The one-take tolerances

Perfection is the enemy of finished. These tolerances are enough:

| Flaw | Tolerable? | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A "um" here and there | Yes | Nobody cares. Ship it. |
| Saying a word twice | Yes | Leave it. |
| A 10-second dead search for a file | Borderline | Trim that bit only. |
| Wrong name for a button you must correct on screen | Yes (note below) | Say "the label may differ" and keep going. |
| Framing slightly off | Yes | Learning happens through the screen content. |
| Digging a minute into a tangential story | No | Record the segment again on that point. |

The "note below" row means: keep it. Your beginner viewers need to see that "the label may
differ" is normal — it is the single most reassuring sentence in software teaching.

## Record the way you outlined

- One video per outline block. Name files `01-hook.mp4`, `02-foundation.mp4`
  — matching your outline table, so the lesson page builds itself.
- Speak the promise in the first 30 seconds and the finish line in the last
  30 seconds of the *hook* video. Viewers decide early.
- If you flounder: stop, take a breath, count three, restart the sentence —
  do not restart the video. Trim later, or leave it; the breath reads as
  composure.
- Script *openings and closings only*. Improvise the middle from your
  outline steps. Fully scripted teaching sounds asleep.

## Export, once, plainly

- 1080p or 720p MP4 (H.264) is universal. Higher resolutions double file
  size and help nobody.
- Keep the raw files in a dated folder. Do not delete. Storage is cheap and
  re-records are expensive.
- Captions: write them by hand for the hook video at minimum — the first
  thirty seconds carry inclusion, and auto-captions mangle names. Full
  human captions for everything is a kindness, not a requirement for v1.

## When a take consistently fails

If you have restarted one segment more than three times, the *script* is
the problem, not you. Shorten the step, split it into two, or lower the
difficulty. The outline exists to make recording boring — boring recording
is a feature.

## Exercise (15 minutes)

1. Run the room check in your two candidate rooms. Clap. Listen.
2. Record a 60-second test video: say the promise sentence and show one
   screen action. Watch it once at 1×. Note one fix, not five.
3. Re-record the 60 seconds with that one fix. That file style is your
   standard. Start `01-hook.mp4` on your real recording day.
