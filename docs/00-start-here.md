# Start Here

You wrote a book, or a manual, or a seriously long set of instructions. It
already solves a problem for people. Good. This guide shows you how to turn a
**slice of it** into a small course you host yourself — without a film crew, a
marketing funnel, or a monthly subscription to a platform that quietly eats
your margin.

By the end of this guide you will have shipped:

- One lesson that makes one promise and keeps it.
- An outline paced so a normal reader can actually finish it.
- Recordings made with the phone and laptop you already own.
- A lesson page and one printable that hand-deliver value before anyone buys.
- Fee math you did on paper, so you pick your sales venue on purpose.
- A refund policy you are proud to publish.
- A plain newsletter drip that announces and onboards without a giant tool.

## Who this is for

You are like me: an author who explains things "like a friend would." Plain
English, step by step, never condescending. You would rather ship something
small and true than a polished course that takes a year. You like owning your
own stuff — your files, your pages, your list.

## How this guide is different from my other guides

- **Selling Digital Products Guide** covers the *breadth* of digital goods —
  ebooks, presets, templates, bundles, and everything in between. This guide
  does one product shape (a small self-hosted course) and does it end to end.
- **Guide-Writing Cookbook** covers *writing the manual itself* — structure,
  voice, drafting. This guide assumes a manual exists and turns it into a
  taught experience.

If you have no manual yet, start with the Cookbook. If your manual is done and
you want to sell digital things generally, the Selling guide is your map. If
you want to *teach* one slice of it and host it yourself, you are in the right
place.

## The whole recipe on one napkin

1. Choose one chapter-worth of promise: a specific reader, a specific outcome,
   a finish line they believe they can reach.
2. Outline it at a pace a tired reader can finish in a week of small sittings.
3. Film it once, plainly, with good-enough audio.
4. Build one lesson page plus one printable that prove the promise is real.
5. Do the fee math and choose where it lives: your own site or a marketplace.
6. Publish a refund policy you would feel good about receiving as a buyer.
7. Write three small emails that drip the course to your plain little list.
